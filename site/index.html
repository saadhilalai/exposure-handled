    Exposure, handled.

    Illustration. A rendered scene, not footage. The real agent reads the Sony cinema camera's S-Log3 live view over USB and, by default, only changes ISO. Aperture, shutter and WB need --allow-exposure.

    Meet the crew inside.

    A picture of the idea. The real agent is one Python loop that runs on every live-view frame, about 24 a second on the Sony cinema camera (live test, about 10 ms per frame to see): see (OpenCV), decide (rules for your look), check (approved ranges), change (Sony's SDK). Every change is logged with time, old value, new value and reason.

    Too bright? A third of a stop down.

    ISO6400
    Real, live test, Sony cinema camera. Starting 2 stops too bright, the agent stepped ISO 6400 → 5000 → 4000 → 3200 → 2500 → 2000, one third of a stop every ~1.7 s, until the scene sat inside its window (−1 to +1 stop). Each change was confirmed by the camera in 116–178 ms.

    Sky clipping? It closes the iris.

    IRISF2.8
    Approved range f/2.8 to f/11, off unless you pass --allow-exposure. Each look sets its own aperture: Cinematic holds f/2.8, Run and gun works f/4–f/8. Changes go in third stops and are logged.

    Wrong light? It repaints the color.

    WB3200K
    White balance: Auto or a Kelvin value only, 2800–7500 K. Presets and Custom stay blocked. When the light is warmer than the limit, the dashboard says so instead of guessing.

    One press. Everything stops.

    0MS
    Real: 33 ms from key press to stopped, measured in the live test on the Sony cinema camera. In the app the kill switch is SPACE, a button on the dashboard, or tools/kill_switch.py. After a stop, the bridge locks: nothing changes until you hand control back.

      How the simulator works. Exposure follows ISO × shutter ÷ f-stop². The reel spins at 0.9 turns a second, so slower shutters smear it. Depth of field uses an 85 mm lens focused at 1.6 m, and the bokeh is shaped by a nine-blade iris. White balance tints by black-body color temperature, noise grows with ISO, and the sensor clips 6 stops over middle grey. The AI here is a small copy of the agent's rules for the demo: 180° shutter, the iris only when ISO can't go lower, ISO to land the subject, and WB to the light (kept warm at sunset). It stays inside the real approved ranges.

      Beat the AI.

      Score: 100, minus points for exposure more than ¼ stop from the target, clipped highlights, white balance error (in mireds), a shutter far from 180°, and ISO above 12800. The AI gets the same starting shot and must stay inside its approved ranges, so a bright scene can beat it.

      Some buttons it will never touch.

      The rules, from the code. Every change goes through one safety function with approved ranges, and is logged with time, old value, new value and reason. ISO 800–12800 is always on. Aperture f/2.8–f/11, shutter 1/48–1/100 and WB 2800–7500 K need --allow-exposure. Focus: none. Never: format, delete, firmware, recording format, codec, frame rate, resolution, picture profile, media settings, or starting and stopping recording. It snapshots your settings at the start and offers to restore them.
